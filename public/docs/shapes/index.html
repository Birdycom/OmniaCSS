    Shapes & Clipping

    CSS class _shapes.scss shapes/index.html 0 KB JavaScriptBaselinev0.3.0

    Clip elements to geometric shapes with clip-path, and let text flow around a floated shape with shape-outside. The .clip-shape-* classes use the newer shape() function for responsive curves. See MDN: clip-path.

    Clip path — basic shapes

    Basic shapes and reference boxes. Everything outside the shape is hidden — including a normal border. For a border that follows the shape, wrap the element in .clip-border-* (see below).

    What it does: clip-path hides everything outside a shape; the hidden part no longer paints or receives pointer events. When to use: non-rectangular images and cards, diagonal section edges, reveal animations. Gotchas: clipping also cuts off the border, outline and box-shadow (draw the border with the .clip-border-* wrapper instead); percentages refer to the element's own box; and a clipped element still occupies its full rectangle in the layout. Use mask instead when you need soft, anti-aliased or gradient edges. See MDN: clip-path.

    ClassCSS
    .clip-noneclip-path: none
    .clip-circleclip-path: circle(50%)
    .clip-circle-0clip-path: circle(0% at 50% 50%) (hidden; start of a reveal)
    .clip-circle-coverclip-path: circle(75% at 50% 50%) (covers the corners; end of a reveal, animate with .transition-clip)
    .clip-ellipseclip-path: ellipse(50% 40%)
    .clip-inset-4clip-path: inset(4px)
    .clip-inset-8clip-path: inset(8px round 8px)
    .clip-border-boxclip-path: border-box
    .clip-padding-boxclip-path: padding-box
    .clip-content-boxclip-path: content-box

    Clip border

    A normal border is cut away by clip-path. Put .clip-border-* on a wrapper around the clipped element: stacked drop-shadow() filters draw an outline that follows the exact shape (polygon, circle or shape()). The color is the wrapper's text color (e.g. .text-dodgerblue-20); the clipped element needs an opaque background.

    ClassCSS
    .clip-borderfilter: drop-shadow(±1px) × 4 — 1px border in currentcolor
    .clip-border-2filter: drop-shadow(±2px) × 4 — 2px border in currentcolor
    .clip-border-4filter: drop-shadow(±4px) × 4 — 4px border in currentcolor

    The first hexagon has a normal 6px border: the clip cuts it away except on the flat top and bottom. The other three use a .clip-border-* wrapper, and the outline follows all six sides.

    Shape outside & shape margin

    Wraps inline content around a floated element. Combine with a float class (.float-left) and usually with the matching .clip-* class so the visual matches the wrap.

    ClassCSS
    .shape-noneshape-outside: none
    .shape-circleshape-outside: circle(50%)
    .shape-ellipseshape-outside: ellipse(50% 40%)
    .shape-margin-boxshape-outside: margin-box
    .shape-border-boxshape-outside: border-box
    .shape-padding-boxshape-outside: padding-box
    .shape-content-boxshape-outside: content-box
    .shape-m-0shape-margin: 0
    .shape-m-1shape-margin: 0.25rem
    .shape-m-2shape-margin: 0.5rem
    .shape-m-4shape-margin: 1rem

    What it does: shape-outside changes the area that inline content has to avoid around a floated element, from the default rectangle to a circle, ellipse, polygon, inset, or even the alpha channel of an image or gradient. shape-margin adds breathing room between the shape and the text. When to use: editorial layouts, pull-quotes and avatars with text flowing around them. Gotchas: the element must be floated and have an explicit width and height (a circle of 50% is relative to that box); the shape only affects text, not the visual, so pair it with a matching clip-path; and there must be enough text next to the float, otherwise you do not see the wrap. See MDN: shape-outside and MDN: shape-margin.

    Look at the left edge of the lines next to the circle: each line starts a bit further in or out, so the text follows the curve with 1rem of room.

    Circle: float-left + shape-circle + clip-circle

    .float-left .shape-circle .clip-circle .m-1
    Follow the left edge of the lines next to the circle: it traces the curve, 1rem away from it.
    A floated circle with shape-outside lets the text follow its curve. Look at where each line starts: close to the circle at its middle, and further to the left near its top and bottom. Without .shape-circle the text would stop at a straight edge, as if the circle were still a square. The class .clip-circle makes the box look round, and .shape-circle makes the text treat it as round, so you need both. The margin of 1rem around the float (.m-1) becomes the gap between the circle and the words, because circle(50%) is measured on the margin box. Once the text passes the bottom of the circle, the lines use the full width of the column again, like the last lines of this paragraph.

    Other shapes: ellipse, polygon, inset and gradient

    Use the classes for the common shapes and an inline shape-outside for anything custom. A polygon makes text follow a diagonal edge (a triangle floated left makes lines longer as you go down); inset(… round …) follows rounded corners; and a gradient or image makes text wrap around every pixel whose alpha is above shape-image-threshold.

    Text around an ellipse, a triangle, a rounded box and a gradient

    .shape-ellipse + .clip-ellipse
    The left edge of the lines follows the curve of a wide oval.
    The text follows the curve of a wide oval. Each line starts a little after the edge of the shape, so the left edge of the text copies the outline. The lines are short near the middle of the oval and longer near its top and bottom. Below the shape the lines use the full width again.
    shape-outside: polygon()
    A triangle: the lines get shorter as the shape gets wider.
    A triangle floated left makes the lines shorter as you go down, because the shape gets wider at the bottom. The text keeps the same distance from the slanted edge all the way down. Below the shape the lines use the full width again, so you can see where the triangle ends.
    .shape-border-box + .round-12
    A box with large round corners: only the lines at the corners move in. The dashed line is the 1rem shape margin.
    Here the text follows a box with large round corners. Next to the straight middle part, all lines start at the same place. Only the lines next to the top and bottom corners move in a little, along the curve. Below the shape the lines use the full width again.
    shape-outside: radial-gradient()
    The shape comes from a gradient: text avoids every part that is not transparent.
    The shape comes from a gradient. Text wraps around every part of it that is not transparent, here a circle. The same works with a picture that has a transparent background, so the words can follow a real outline. Below the shape the lines use the full width again.

    Float right and the reference boxes

    .float-right works mirrored. The reference-box classes (.shape-margin-box, .shape-border-box, .shape-padding-box, .shape-content-box) pick which edge of a box with a border radius the text follows.

    float-right + shape-border-box with rounded corners

    .float-right .shape-border-box .shape-m-4 .round-full
    The text is justified, so every line ends exactly at the shape: follow the right ends, they bend around the dashed ring (the 1rem shape margin). Under the circle they run full width again.
    Float right works the same way, mirrored. The shape here is a plain box with the class .round-full and the class .shape-border-box, so the text follows the round border edge and the right ends of these lines bend around the circle. No clip-path is needed, because the border radius already makes the box round. .shape-m-4 keeps 1rem of room, and the margin on the left of the float (.ml-1.5) must be at least that big, because the shape margin cannot reach past the margin box. This is handy for a photo or a logo at the side of an article. The lines under the shape run across the full width again.

Hover or focus the cards: the left one slides open from inset(0 72% 0 0), the middle one grows a circle, the right one grows a polygon. The label sits under the clipped part, so it is never cut.
