Skip ink: why a line can look broken

By default the browser leaves a small gap in an underline wherever a letter hangs below the line, such as g, j, p, q and y. That keeps links easy to read, but a wavy or thick line then looks broken. Compare the three tiles: the first skips, the second sets text-decoration-skip-ink: none, the third moves the line below the letters with .underline-under.

skip-ink: auto (default)
The line stops at p, p and g.

Shipping

skip-ink: none
One continuous line; it runs through the letters.

Shipping

.underline-under
The line sits below the lowest letter, so nothing is in its way.

Thickness

The number is the thickness in tenths of the font size: .decoration-1 is 0.1em, .decoration-3 is 0.3em. Because it is in em, the line grows with the text. .decoration-auto lets the font decide.

.decoration-auto
The thickness the font itself suggests.

Underline offset

Move the underline away from the text. Classes 5 to 9 push it down in steps of 0.1em; classes 1 to 4 are negative and pull it up into the letters, which is only useful for special effects.

Color, and putting it together

Give the line its own color with .decoration-{color}. Two everyday uses: a red wavy line that marks a spelling mistake, and a link that only shows its line when you point at it. Hover the link to try it.

.underline .decoration-wavy .decoration-red
A red wavy line marks a spelling mistake.

Browser support

All current browsers support these properties. text-decoration-skip-spaces and text-decoration-inset are newer; browsers without them simply draw the normal line.
