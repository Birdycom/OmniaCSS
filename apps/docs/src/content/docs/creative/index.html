Creative CSS

Showcase _css_functions.scss creative/index.html v0.8.2

Effects people usually reach for JavaScript or images to build, made with CSS and the Omnia tokens only: animated gradient borders (@property), neon buttons, 3D flip cards, glassmorphism, text fills, typing and wave text, clip-path reveals, a scroll-driven reading bar, a theme switch with :has(), retro 3D type and loaders. Every effect respects prefers-reduced-motion.

The examples are built with OmniaCSS classes such as .border-conic-spin, .shadow-neon, .glass, .animate-typing and .text-shadow-3d, with variants such as hover:, before: and group-hover:. The few lines of CSS in a sample are custom: the size of that specimen, or the switch, which is a component. Each sample says which part that is.

Animated glowing border

The OmniaCSS class .border-conic-spin turns a conic gradient round the border (a registered @property angle) and puts a blurred copy behind it as the glow. Tune it with --oc-conic-colors, --oc-conic-speed, --oc-conic-blur and --oc-conic-fill.

.border-conic-spin
Runs by itself: the colors travel round the border, and a blurred copy of the ring glows behind it.

Wave text with sibling-index()

One animation, .animate-wave, and the OmniaCSS .animate-delay-stagger utility gives every letter its own delay from its position. No nth-child list, no inline delays. --oc-wave-color is the color at the top of the jump.

Scroll-driven reading progress

The bar is the OmniaCSS preset .animate-scroll-progress, driven by scroll instead of time. .timeline-scroll points it at the nearest scroll box instead of the page. Scroll the text box. More on Scroll animations.

.animate-scroll-progress .timeline-scroll
Scroll the text box: the blue bar at its top grows from 0 to 100% while you read.

Scroll this box. The bar at the top grows from 0 to 100% while you read.

It is a normal @keyframes animation, but its progress comes from the scroll position of the nearest scroll container instead of a clock.

On a real page use .animate-scroll-progress on its own: it follows the scroll of the whole page.

Browsers without scroll-driven animations show the bar full width and still.

No scroll listeners, no requestAnimationFrame, and it runs off the main thread.

End of the article.

Day/night switch with :has()

A real checkbox with .appearance-none; its ::before is the thumb. The panel switches theme through :has(.cr-switch:checked). A switch is a component, not a utility, so this example keeps its own CSS; OmniaUI has it ready-made as .oc-switch (and .oc-theme-switch for a light, dark or system choice).

appearance: none + :has(.cr-switch:checked)
Click the switch: the sun turns into a moon and the panel goes from day to night.

Loaders

A conic gradient (.bg-conic with .from-* and .to-*) cut into a ring by .mask-ring and turned by .animate-spin; and dots that beat one after another: .animate-beat plus .animate-delay-stagger .stagger-150, which computes the delay from sibling-index(). Without sibling-index() (Firefox) the dots beat together.

.bg-conic .mask-ring .animate-spin
Runs by itself: a gradient cut into a ring by a mask, turning once a second.
.animate-beat .animate-delay-stagger
Runs by itself: with .stagger-150 each dot beats 150 ms after the one before it.

Browser support

Everything here works in current browsers. Without scroll-driven animations (Firefox) the reading bar shows full width and stands still; without sibling-index() (Firefox) every letter and dot starts at the same moment.
