Margin — what it does (box model)

Margin is the space outside the border: it pushes the element away from its neighbours and parent. The striped orange area is the margin, exactly like the box-model diagram in the browser DevTools.

What it does: margin sets the space outside an element's border. It pushes neighbours away and never gets the element's background. When to use: spacing between siblings, centering a block with .m-auto, nudging a single element. For spacing between many children, gap on a flex or grid parent is usually cleaner. Gotchas: vertical margins of adjacent blocks collapse (the larger one wins instead of adding up), margins of a first/last child can leak through a parent without padding or border, and auto only centers when the element has a width. See MDN: margin and margin collapsing.

Centering with auto

.m-auto and .mh-auto split the free inline space equally on both sides. The element needs a width smaller than its container, otherwise there is no free space to share.

.mh-auto + width: 8rem
content
equal orange area left and right
no width
content
block fills the container: nothing to center
<div class="mh-auto" style="inline-size: 8rem">Centered</div>

Gotcha: vertical margins collapse

The top box has .mb-2 (2rem) and the bottom box has .mt-1 (1rem). You might expect a 3rem gap, but adjacent vertical margins merge into the larger one: the gap is 2rem. Horizontal margins and flex/grid items never collapse.
