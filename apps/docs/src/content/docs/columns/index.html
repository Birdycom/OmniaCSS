Multi-column Layout

CSS class _layout_flow.scss columns/index.html v0.3.0

Flow content into newspaper-style columns. Use .cols-* for a fixed number of columns or .col-w-* for an ideal column width; the gap utilities (.gap-*) set the space between them. The older .columns-* shorthand classes stay available. See MDN: Multi-column layout.

What it does: multi-column layout flows one block of content into several columns, like a newspaper: the browser decides where each column breaks. When to use: long lists, tag clouds, short articles, link directories. Gotchas: columns balance their height by default (.col-fill-balance), so a fixed-height container fills the first column first only with .col-fill-auto; a heading can span all columns with .col-span-all; and content is read top to bottom per column, which is poor for long text on small screens. Prefer .col-w-* (a minimum width) over a fixed count so the number of columns adapts. See MDN: columns and MDN: column-rule.

One short article in three columns with a thin rule between them. The text runs down the first column, then continues at the top of the next. On a phone three columns get too narrow; the next example shows the better choice there.

.cols-3 .col-rule-solid .col-rule-1
Three columns with a thin line between them. The text runs down the first column, then goes on at the top of the next, also in the middle of a sentence.
Company 1 opens a second office this spring. The new building is close to the station, so most visitors can come by train. There is room for twelve desks, two meeting rooms and a small kitchen.
Customers can visit from Monday to Friday, between nine and five. Please book a meeting room at least one day ahead. The old office stays open as well.
Both offices share one phone number and one email address. If you are not sure which office to visit, ask us first and we will tell you which team works where.
Parking is limited, so we suggest the train or a bike. There are bike racks next to the main door. Coffee and tea are free for every visitor.
<div class="cols-3 col-rule-solid col-rule-1" style="column-gap: 2rem"> <p>Company 1 opens a second office this spring. …</p> <p>Customers can visit from Monday to Friday, …</p> <p>…</p> </div>

With .col-w-16 you ask for columns of about 16rem and the browser decides how many fit. Drag the handle in the bottom-right corner of the box to make it narrower: at about 34rem the two columns become one. On a wide screen there would be three or more. No media queries needed.

.col-w-16
Columns of about 16rem; the browser decides how many fit. Drag the bottom-right corner of the box to resize it.
Every column here is about 16rem wide. When the box gets narrower, a column drops off and the text reflows into the ones that are left.
This keeps the lines at a comfortable length on every screen. Wide screens get more columns, a phone gets one.
That is why a width is usually a better choice than a fixed count. The count only works well when you know how wide the box will be.
<div class="col-w-16"> <p>Every column here is about 16rem wide. …</p> <p>…</p> </div>

Browser support

Multi-column layout is broadly supported; without support content simply flows in a single column.
